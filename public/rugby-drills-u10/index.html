What an U10 grade is allowed to do

U10 adds the ruck, the maul and an uncontested scrum to the tackle your grade learned at U9. The lineout is not part of U10 and does not appear until U12. Nothing on this page assumes otherwise, so a lineout drill will not show up in an U10 session by accident.

Rucks, mauls and the uncontested scrum arrive. The lineout does not, that waits for U12. The RFU sets this out in Regulation 15 rather than leaving it to a coach's judgement. The U10 rules of play are the version that counts to a referee, they are reissued every season, so check yours there rather than trusting a copy on somebody else's website. Ours included.

What to work on at U10

  • What happens immediately after the tackle. The tackle itself is last year's work. Placing the ball, rolling away and arriving on your feet is this year's.
  • Arriving low and staying on your feet. The two commonest U10 penalties in one sentence.
  • Scrum shape without any push. Three players, bound properly, going nowhere. It is duller than they want it to be and it is the bit that keeps necks safe.
  • Not losing the handling. Twenty-five of these drills are still passing. The squad that only rucks all winter is a squad that cannot move a ball in March.

U10 is the grade where a session plan starts genuinely mattering. There is more to cover than there are Tuesdays, so a night that drifts costs you something real. The six ready-made U10 sessions here each pick one theme and stay on it, because covering rucking, scrums and handling properly in one hour is not a thing anybody has managed.

Common questions

What can U10s do that U9s cannot?

Ruck and maul, plus an uncontested scrum. U9 is tackle only. The step from one to the other is the largest single jump in the minis game, which is why an U10 season tends to feel busier than any other.

Can U10s have a lineout?

No. The lineout arrives at U12 under RFU Regulation 15. U10 and U11 play without one, so restarts and free kicks are worth more of your session time than most coaches give them.

How long is an U10 rugby session?

An hour of activity is the usual booking and it is enough if you pick one theme and stay on it. The U10 sessions here run between 45 and 60 minutes with the water breaks already counted, because the minutes you forget are always the ones that make you overrun.

What should an U10 rugby session include?

One theme, a warm-up that involves a ball, a decent block on whatever the theme is, then a game that makes them use it. Rucking taught in isolation for an hour does not survive contact with a Sunday morning.
