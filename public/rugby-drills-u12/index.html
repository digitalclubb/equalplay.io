What an U12 grade is allowed to do

U12 is the first grade with a lineout, on top of everything U10 and U11 already had. It is as close to the full catalogue as any grade gets: 103 of the 104 drills here are legal at U12. The one that is not is a tag drill written for U7 and U8, which by now they have long outgrown.

The lineout arrives. This is the last year of minis and it looks close to the full game. The RFU sets this out in Regulation 15 rather than leaving it to a coach's judgement. The U12 rules of play are the version that counts to a referee, they are reissued every season, so check yours there rather than trusting a copy on somebody else's website. Ours included.

What to work on at U12

  • The lineout, from the ground up. Nobody has done one before. Start with a throw that goes straight before anybody leaves the floor.
  • Defence that holds its shape. U12 attackers are quick enough to punish a line that drifts.
  • Winning the ball back. The jackal, taught properly and legally, changes more U12 games than any attacking move.
  • Moving the ball early. The step up to U13 punishes a squad that carries into contact every time.

U12 gets very nearly the whole catalogue, which is a mixed blessing. There is genuinely more available than a season has room for, so the useful question stops being what you could coach and becomes what you are going to leave out. The 75 minute sessions here exist because U12 is the first grade where an hour honestly is not enough for a set piece night.

Common questions

What age does the lineout start in rugby?

U12. RFU Regulation 15 introduces the uncontested lineout at U12, so it is the last major part of the game to arrive in minis rugby. U10 and U11 play without one.

What is different about U12 rugby?

The lineout arrives and the game starts to resemble the adult version. It is also the last year of minis, so a fair amount of an U12 season is quietly about being ready for U13.

How long should an U12 session be?

An hour still works for a single theme. A set piece night needs 75 minutes, because scrum and lineout in the same session cannot be rushed safely. Both lengths are in the ready-made U12 sessions here.
