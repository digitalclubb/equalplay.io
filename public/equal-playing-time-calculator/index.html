How the calculation works

There is only one sum involved. Multiply the players on the pitch by the length of the match and you have the total pool of player-minutes available to give out:

Total player-minutes = players on the pitch × minutes of play

Each player's fair share = total player-minutes ÷ players available

So a squad of 14 playing a 50 minute match with 10 on the pitch has 500 player-minutes to share, which is 35.7 minutes each and about 14 minutes each on the side.

Count the whole session rather than one match. Minis fixtures in particular are usually several short games and a child who sat out most of game one is only treated fairly if game three knows about it.

When the numbers don't divide evenly

They usually won't. Chasing an exact split inside one match tends to produce silly substitutions anyway. Two things help more:

  • Balance across the session or the season. Whoever came up short last week gets first call this week. This is what fixes the child who always seems to be the one rounded down.
  • Track it rather than trusting your memory. The difference between "everyone got roughly the same" and knowing that Ffion played 34 minutes and Sam played 36 is the difference between a defensible answer and an argument on the touchline.

Playing time minimums

Equal playing time and required playing time are different things. Age grade rugby sets minimum and maximum minutes per age group in RFU Regulation 15. See Regulation 15 playing time for coaches for how to check yours and make sure every child clears it.

Common questions

How do you calculate equal playing time?

Multiply the number of players on the pitch by the length of the match to get the total player-minutes available, then divide by the number of players in your squad. That is each child's fair share.

How many minutes does each player get with a squad of 14 and 10 on the pitch?

In a 50 minute match with 10 players on the pitch there are 500 player-minutes to share. Divided between 14 players that is about 35.7 minutes each, so each child spends roughly 14 minutes off the pitch.

Does equal playing time mean everyone plays the same amount every match?

Not necessarily. When a squad does not divide evenly it is usually fairer to balance game time across a whole session or season, giving a child who was short last time first call this time, rather than forcing every single match to come out level.
