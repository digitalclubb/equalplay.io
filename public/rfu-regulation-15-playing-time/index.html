Regulation 15 playing time: a coach's guide

Age grade rugby in England caps how long a child may play and sets a minimum they should get. Here is how the rules are structured, where to find the numbers for your age group and how to make sure every child in your squad actually reaches the minimum.

Check the current figures against the RFU directly. Regulation 15 is revised between seasons and the exact minutes differ by age group. We deliberately don't reproduce the table here, because a stale copy on a third-party site is worse than no copy at all. Use the official Regulation 15 page and the appendix for your age group.

What Regulation 15 is

Regulation 15 is the RFU regulation covering age grade rugby. Every player from Under 6 through to Under 18. It sets out the rules of play for each year group, the progressions from tag through to the full contact game and the limits on how much rugby a child may play.

The regulation itself is a relatively short document. The detail that matters to a coach on match day lives in the appendices, one per age group, each giving that year's rules of play, numbers per side, pitch size and playing time.

How playing time is governed

There are two separate limits and coaches routinely only think about one of them.

The maximum

A cap on how much rugby a child may play in a single day and it counts training as well as matches. This is the one that catches out festivals and tournaments, where a squad can quietly rack up game after game across a morning. It is a welfare limit, not a target.

The minimum

A floor on how much a child who has turned up should actually get. This is the one that gets missed, because nothing on the pitch reminds you about it. Nobody notices the child who played eight minutes; they notice the score.

Finding the figures for your age group

  1. Go to the RFU's Regulation 15 page.
  2. Open the appendix for your age group. They are published as separate rules of play documents, numbered by age.
  3. Check the effective date at the top. If it isn't the current season, you've got an old copy.

A child's age group is determined by how old they are at midnight on 31 August, so a player's age grade holds for the whole season regardless of when their birthday falls.

Outside England? Regulation 15 is an RFU regulation and applies to English age grade rugby. Wales, Scotland and Ireland each publish their own age grade law variations through the WRU, Scottish Rugby and the IRFU. The principle is the same everywhere, though the minutes differ.

Knowing the rule is the easy bit

Most coaches can find out that their age group has a minimum. Hitting it, for fourteen children, across four short games, while two of them arrived late and one has gone off with a dead leg, is a different problem. It is arithmetic, done badly, in the cold.

What tends to go wrong:

  • Counting matches instead of minutes. "She played in three of the four games" says nothing about how long she was on for.
  • Losing track after a disruption. A late arrival or an injury resets everyone's mental sums and the plan on the back of the team sheet is now wrong.
  • The same children rounded down. When a squad doesn't divide evenly somebody has to come up short. Without a record it's the same quiet child every week.
  • Only checking at the end. By the time you add it up in the car park it's too late to fix.
