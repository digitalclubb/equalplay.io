# Design context

Written down so design work does not have to guess. Confirmed by the project owner,
who coaches the U10s the hub was built for.

## Who uses it

Volunteer youth rugby coaches in England, mostly parents. One or two coaches per age
group at a community club. They are not sports scientists and they are not being paid.
Most have a full-time job and get the session plan together on a Monday night.

## Where they use it

Two very different places. The design has to serve both:

- **At a kitchen table**, Sunday or Monday night, putting a session together for
  Tuesday. Unhurried, probably a phone, possibly a laptop.
- **At the side of a pitch**, mid-session, one-handed, in the rain, wearing a coat,
  often with no signal. Glancing rather than reading. This is the harder case and it
  wins any argument about layout.

## What they are trying to do

1. Find drills their age grade is actually allowed to do. This is the whole point.
   RFU Regulation 15 introduces tackling at U9, rucks and scrums at U10 and no
   lineout at any minis grade. Getting it wrong is a safety matter rather than a
   mistake.
2. Put a training session together that fits the time they have booked on the pitch.
3. Know what to put in the kit bag.
4. Read the thing back at the pitch without needing signal.

## How it should feel

Like a local dad passing on what he has learned. Not like a product.

- Plain, direct, unhurried. No selling. Contractions in prose, because without
  them it reads like a form letter.
- Confident about safety and never squeamish about it. The safety note is the loudest
  thing on a contact drill on purpose.
- Never patronising. These are adults giving up their Sundays.
- Quiet rather than exciting. Nobody needs a dashboard.

## Brand

Established by the free rotation planner at `/planner`. The homepage and the hub
inherit it:

- Navy `#000537` for chrome, brand orange `#e75333` for the mark, a darker
  `#cf3918` for interactive text so it passes contrast
- Outfit for display, DM Sans for body, both already loaded
- Tokens live in `src/base.css` and nothing hard-codes a colour
- Navy is chrome, never text. `--color-navy` does not flip with the colour scheme,
  so text on any surface that does flip uses `--color-text`. Checked by
  `e2e/contrast.spec.ts` in both schemes, because it has been got wrong twice
- Mobile-first. No framework, no CSS framework, vanilla DOM

## Non-negotiables

- Works offline once opened. The service worker and static content exist for this.
- Nothing about any child is ever stored. Not names, not notes, not photos.
- 320px is a supported width. Test there, not just at 390.
- Touch targets 44px minimum. Cold wet hands.
- Age gating is a safety feature and is never presented as a preference.

## Quality bar

Shipping to real coaches at a real club, so production quality. Not a flagship
marketing site. Restraint over decoration: it is a tool people use in bad weather.
